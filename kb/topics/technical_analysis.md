# Technical analysis

What the classic books and the research say about trend, momentum and chart patterns, in our words.

## Core ideas we use
- **Trade with the higher-timeframe trend, enter on a pullback.** Elder's triple screen uses three timeframes: a trend filter, an oscillator for the pullback, and a stop-order entry. Our tests found the trend filter the most useful part. — Alexander Elder, *Trading for a Living*, Wiley, 1993.
- **Simple, rule-based futures trading with protective stops, no averaging down.** — Jim Wyckoff, *Trading Smart*, 2003.
- **The standard toolkit:** trend lines, support and resistance, moving averages, oscillators, volume, and classic chart patterns, with the warning that indicators confirm price rather than lead it. — John J. Murphy, *Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets*, New York Institute of Finance, 1999.
- **Systems need definitions, costs and testing.** Breakout systems (such as Donchian channels), trend systems and reversion systems are worth testing only with exact rules and realistic costs. — Perry J. Kaufman, *Trading Systems and Methods*, Wiley (5th ed., 2013).
- **Pattern statistics vary a lot by pattern and market.** Treat any single pattern's reputation with caution until it's measured on your own data. — Thomas N. Bulkowski, *Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns*, Wiley (2nd ed., 2005).

## What the research says
- **Patterns can carry information, but information isn't profit.** Using smoothed prices and objective pattern definitions, some classic patterns changed the distribution of next-day returns on US stocks, especially Nasdaq stocks. That doesn't show trading profits after costs. — Lo, Mamaysky & Wang, "Foundations of Technical Analysis," *Journal of Finance*, 2000. https://www.nber.org/papers/w7613
- **Support and resistance as optimal stopping.** A support line is worth trading only while the favourable regime lasts, and that regime fades, which supports a time stop. — Henderson, Jacka, Liu & Maeda, 2021/2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.02331
- **Momentum and attention drive crypto returns,** and both are behavioural. — Yukun Liu & Aleh Tsyvinski, "Risks and Returns of Cryptocurrency," *Review of Financial Studies*, 2021. https://www.nber.org/papers/w24877
- **Momentum is weak in the smallest assets.** In crypto, momentum works for larger coins and fails in the smallest ones. — Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu, "Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency," *Journal of Finance*, 2022. https://www.nber.org/papers/w25882

## For the desk
- Write every pattern as exact rules before testing. Count each threshold as a variant.
- Prefer a higher-timeframe trend filter plus a simple entry. That combination is what survived our tests (results.md).
